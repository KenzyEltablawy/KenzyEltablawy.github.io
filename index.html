BLOOMS: Event-Driven, Tick-Free NPCs in Unreal Engine 5.8

Software Design / Engineering Design (SFTD) · Unreal Engine 5.8 · C++

I am a junior Unreal Engine 5 developer, and BLOOMS is the project where I went deepest into C++ for performance. In Unreal, NPCs normally tick: the engine runs their code every frame, even when they are just standing still. I asked a simple question: can an NPC that runs only on events still behave socially and animate smoothly, and how little CPU time can it get away with? To find out, I built a small city where Citizens greet the player, get uncomfortable if you stand too close, then confront, warn and walk away. Ticking is switched off in my own code, and the animation runs on a custom C++ proxy instead of an AnimGraph.

The poster

  • Problem: a standing NPC is asked “anything to do?” sixty times a second, and the answer is almost always no. A Blueprint tick adds the Blueprint virtual machine on top.
  • Method: two drawings: the Citizen brain that only reacts to events, and the ten-step social ladder where every step starts from an event.
  • Experiment: three builds, one counter. A packaged build, t.MaxFPS 0, r.VSync 0, the biggest value in a 30 s watch, three trials per build and count.
  • Results, limits and recommendations are written in plain numbers. Where I cannot tell, I say so.

Deep C++ for performance — what I built

  • A brain that never ticks. The Citizen brain is a UHumanoidActorComponent with tick flags off. Nothing I wrote is visited every frame.
  • Events only. Two overlap spheres (250 cm interaction, 150 cm comfort), one-shot FTimerHandle timers that are cleared before they are reset, and animation-end callbacks drive the whole social ladder: notice, greet, reply, look again, discomfort, confront, last warning, walk away.
  • Animation without an AnimGraph. A custom FAnimInstanceProxy whose Evaluate() runs on the animation worker thread: three cross-fading idles, five walk phases, head look-at clamped at 46° and eyes at 35°.
  • No pop at walk-stop. The exact pose is copied as a snapshot, then blended into the idle, which removed a one-frame jump.
  • Data assets, not code. Animations, audio, distances, blink, and head and eye limits are loaded once at BeginPlay.
  • Precise claim. My behavior layer has no ticks. The engine still ticks movement and skeletal meshes, so “zero tick” never means “the engine does nothing.”

Hypotheses

  • H1 With ticking off, Citizens cost almost nothing even when several animate together. Tested: 0.01 ms for five.
  • H2 Cheaper than the same code with ticking on, and cheaper than a Blueprint version; the gap grows with crowd size.
  • H3 Behavior is identical in every build.
  • H4 My C++ animation proxy costs less than an AnimGraph doing the same job.

Research paper · in preparation

Working title: “What Does It Cost to Make an NPC React? Measuring Event-Driven, Tick-Free Behavior in Unreal Engine 5.8”

Outline: 1 Introduction and research question · 2 Related work (Nystrom 2014; Gregory 2018; Millington & Funge 2009; Iovino et al. 2022; Ripamonti et al. 2017) · 3 Method: the BLOOMS architecture and builds A, B, B2 and C · 4 Results with the average and the biggest value · 5 Limits · 6 Conclusion and future work.

Measurements still to run: Build B (same C++, ticking on), Build B2 (adds a per-frame distance check, so it measures a real polling NPC), Build A (Blueprint with Event Tick and an Animation Blueprint), and an AnimGraph build for H4. Every count re-measured with the average next to the biggest value, a finer counter or Unreal Insights, the 12 behavior checks, and the Animation Budget Allocator on top.

Analysis rule: reduction % = (bigger − smaller) ÷ bigger × 100. If the gap between two builds is smaller than the spread between my own trials, I report “cannot tell apart” and give no percentage.

What BLOOMS is teaching me as a game developer

  • Unreal C++ at engine level: components, overlaps, timers, delegates and reflection.
  • Which work runs on the game thread and which runs on the animation worker thread, and why a game-thread counter may not include it.
  • Profiling in a packaged build: one isolating counter, controlled settings, three trials, and Unreal Insights next.
  • Data-driven design: changing a Citizen without opening C++.
  • Experimental method and technical writing: hypotheses, controls, honest limits, a poster, a talk, and now a paper.
